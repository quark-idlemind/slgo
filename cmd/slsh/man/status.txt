status is about the plumbing rather than about the world: how the
circuit under this session is doing, and what has been crossing it.
Everything else in the shell asks after inventory, objects or people;
this asks after the connection they all arrive over.

It answers for the session this shell is attached to, and it is the
daemon's answer -- a shell that logged in for itself has nothing to ask
and says so.  The heading names the profile, the avatar and the region,
and under it the simulator's own channel version, the packet counters,
how many clients the daemon has attached to this session, and how many
capabilities the simulator granted.

# What the counters are for

They say whether a session that looks idle is idle or broken.  A quiet
region and a dead circuit look identical from the prompt: nothing is
being printed either way.  The counters tell them apart -- packets
still arriving is a quiet region, packets going out and being resent
and then abandoned is a circuit that has stopped being listened to at
the far end.  Duplicates are the region saying something twice, which
is ordinary in small numbers.

The counters belong to the connection the session is on now.  A session
that dropped and was brought back is counting from the new circuit, so
small numbers under a session that has been up for hours are a
reconnection rather than a quiet afternoon.

# The messages with no handler

Anything listed under that heading arrived and this build had nothing
to do with it, commonest first, because the one arriving hundreds of
times is the one worth writing a handler for.  It is also how a
protocol change announces itself: a message that was never sent before
starts appearing, and the count grows.  An empty list is the ordinary
case and prints nothing at all.

# Examples

	status
	status > status-before-the-run
