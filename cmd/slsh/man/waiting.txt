waiting lists everything that wants an answer, numbered.

Seven sorts of thing arrive that way and they are all in one list,
because from where a person sits they are one question: something is
asking, and the answer is yes, no, one of these, or not now.  A
teleport offered, a script asking permission, a blue menu, a text box,
an item somebody is handing over, an offer of friendship, an invitation
into a group.

A viewer draws these and leaves them on the screen until somebody deals
with them.  A shell has no screen to leave them on, so they are
gathered here, the prompt says how many there are, and nothing is
answered by being looked at.

Each line says who is asking and what for, and the line under it says
what may be typed: the buttons a dialog offers, the fee an invitation
wants, or the plain "answer, no, ignore".

# The number belongs to the thing

A number is handed out when a thing is first seen and kept until it
goes, so a number on the screen still means the same thing after
something else has been answered.  Numbering by position looks
identical until the moment it matters -- answer the first of two and
the second becomes 1, and the "answer 2" already decided on is now
either an error or, worse, something else.  The list can therefore look
sparse, which is the honest picture: the thing that was 2 is still 2.
When nothing is left waiting the numbers start again at one.

# Options

-a includes the things ignore has set aside.  Without it they are not
listed and not counted at the prompt, which is what ignore is for.

# Examples

	waiting
	waiting -a

See also: answer, no, ignore, and offers for the older way to the
friendship and inventory offers, which appear here too.
