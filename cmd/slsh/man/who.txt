who lists the other avatars in the region, nearest first.  It is the
people half of what "objects" does for the furniture, and "where" and
"look" answer the two questions either side of it: where this avatar is
standing, and what the region is.

It takes no argument.  Each line is a number, the name, the distance in
metres and the key.  The distance is a straight line through all three
axes rather than a distance across the ground, so somebody a hundred
metres overhead is a hundred metres away and not the nearest person in
the room.  The avatar this shell is driving is not in its own listing.

# The number down the left is meant to be typed

It is not decoration.  Every command that names a person -- im, chat,
give, offer -- takes a number in place of a name, and it means whoever
was on that line of the last listing this shell printed.

That makes it the shell's count rather than the region's, with two
consequences.  A second run renumbers everybody, since somebody who has
walked closer changes places; and a listing from some other command --
friends, lookup -- replaces it, so the number to use is always the one
from the listing still on the screen.

# A name that has not arrived yet

An avatar's name is only ever had by asking, and the answer is given a
few seconds to come back before the listing is printed.  Whoever is
still unanswered appears as the first eight characters of their key, in
brackets.  That is a question in flight rather than a person with no
name, and the next run usually has it.

# Examples

Who is here, and then a word to the second of them:

	who
	im 2 is the lantern by the gate yours?

See also: where, look, objects, lookup and profile for somebody who is
not standing here, and im for talking to one who is.
