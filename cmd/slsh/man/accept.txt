accept says yes to an offer of friendship, or to an item somebody has
handed over.  "decline" is the same question answered the other way,
and both of them are what "offers" lists.

With nothing after it, it takes the only offer waiting.  With something
after it, that names one: part of the name of whoever offered
friendship, or part of the name of the item offered, or the whole name
of whoever sent it.  What is typed is tried against the items first and
only then against the friendship offers, so a word that could be either
takes the item.

Naming settles an ambiguity between friendship offers and does not
settle one between items.  A word matching two friendship offers is
refused and says how many it matched, so nothing is answered by
accident.  Items are matched one at a time and the first hit wins,
oldest first: with two offers waiting under the same item name, naming
it answered the older of them and left the newer sitting there, saying
nothing at all about the one it had not touched.  So an item is worth
naming in full, and the report is worth reading, since it says both the
item and who sent it.

# An item lands in the folder the shell is in

"cd Objects" then "accept" puts it in Objects, and that is the whole
reason to answer an offer here rather than in a viewer: the folder is
chosen rather than left to the grid.  Answering the same offer through
"waiting" and "answer" instead puts it in the Objects folder, since a
number typed at a listing says nothing about where anything should go.

A shell standing in a folder that has since been renamed or removed has
nowhere to accept into, and says so rather than letting the grid
choose.

# Which kind a bare command means

Where one of each is waiting, the bare command keeps its older meaning
and takes the friendship offer; the item has to be named.

Two items and nothing else waiting is refused, but not in a way that
says so: the bare command looks for an item, finds two and declines to
choose, and then falls through to the friendship offers, of which there
are none.  So it answers "no offers waiting" while two of them sit in
"offers" -- measured, with two items and no friendship offer, both
"accept" and "decline" said exactly that.  It is the sentence that is
wrong and not the refusal; naming one of the two is what to do next.

# What it cannot tell you

Whether the item actually arrived.  The grid does the moving and says
nothing about it, so the report means the answer was sent, and looking
in the folder afterwards is what says it is there.

Accepting a friendship also records it here, since the grid tells the
side that offered and tells the side that accepted nothing whatsoever.

# Examples

The only offer waiting:

	accept

One of several, by who sent it or what it is called:

	accept Example
	cd Objects
	accept lantern

See also: decline, offers, waiting and answer for the same offers
alongside everything else that wants an answer, and ls for looking at
what arrived.
