offer asks somebody to be a friend.  It is the other end of the offers
that arrive here: what this sends lands in their viewer as a question,
to be accepted or refused, exactly as theirs lands in `waiting` and
`offers` on this side.

    offer Example Resident

Who is named by a name, by a key, or by the number beside a name in the
last listing.  Anything after the name is the note that goes with the
offer, and with nothing there the shell writes one -- "Would you be my
friend?" -- because an empty note would arrive as a blank line.  That
sentence is what the other side reads, so a note of one's own is
usually worth typing.

As with `im`, the name is the longest run of one or two words at the
front that answers to somebody, and the rest is the note.  A name with
a space in it is therefore taken whole rather than leaving its last
word at the front of the greeting.

## Nothing here says whether it was accepted

The offer goes out and the command reports that it was offered.  When
it is answered, the answer arrives as a notice on the screen, whichever
mode the shell is in -- and the friend list is the other place to look,
since a friendship formed while the session is up is added there as it
happens.

An offer nobody answers stays pending on their side, and sending a
second one does not cancel the first.

## Who a name may be

The session's cache is asked, and then whoever is standing in the
region.  The grid's search is not: this reaches out to somebody, and a
name guessed at wrong offers friendship to a stranger.  `lookup` makes
that search when it is wanted, and the number it prints is what to give
this.

## Examples

    offer Example Resident

With a note, which is what the other side reads:

    offer Another Resident we met at the build last night

See also: `offers`, `accept` and `decline` for the offers coming the
other way, `friends` for the list an accepted one joins, and `lookup`
for finding somebody who is not here.
