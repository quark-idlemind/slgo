map draws the other avatars as characters on a grid.  It is the half of
the question "who" cannot answer: a listing says somebody is twenty
metres away and nothing at all about which way, and three people at
twenty metres are a crowd in one direction and a scattering in another.

There are two pictures.  By default it is the ground around this avatar,
sixty-four metres across, with the avatar in the middle of it.  With
--region it is the whole region -- 256 metres -- with the avatar
wherever in it the avatar really is.

# The picture is wider than it is tall, which is what makes it look square

A character cell is taller than it is wide, so a grid drawn with as many
columns as rows comes out on the screen taller than it is wide, and a
square region looks like a doorway.  That matters more here than it
would in a listing, because the whole point of a picture is that the eye
reads the shape without doing any arithmetic.

So the rows are what is chosen and the columns follow: as many as it
takes for a square of ground to look square, which is the rows
multiplied by the shape of a cell.  How much taller than wide a cell is
belongs to the font somebody reads a terminal in rather than to
anything here, so it is a setting -- map_ratio -- and it is written the
way it is measured, height first.  The default is 7:3, a cell seven
high and three wide, which is what the mono font this was measured in
turned out to be.  Twice as tall as wide was the guess before anybody
measured, and "set map_ratio 2:1" is how to have that back.

With the sixteen rows it draws unless told otherwise, 7:3 is 16 * 7 / 3
= 37 columns, rounded from 37.3: in the region view that is 256/16 = 16
metres to a row and 256/37, a shade under 7, to a column, and in the
close view 4 metres to a row and about 1.7 to a column.

The line under the grid says how much ground the picture covers -- "64m
x 64m" -- because that is the number somebody reads a map for, and
because a picture with no size under it is a drawing.  It is the same
number both ways, which is the point of there being more columns than
rows: the picture is a square of the world however finely it is drawn.
More rows buy detail rather than reach, so the size does not change when
--rows does.

The ratio itself is not printed.  It is a fact about somebody's font and
not about the region, it is the same for every picture they will ever
draw, and the numbers that are printed are the ones a person wanted
when they asked for a map.

# The marks

	*   this avatar
	o   somebody within three metres of this avatar's height
	^   somebody higher than that
	v   somebody lower than that

Three metres is about one storey: an avatar is a couple of metres tall
and a floor of a building is three or so, so an "o" is somebody who
could be walked over to and a "^" is somebody on the roof.  It is the
map_level setting, and whatever it is set to is the number in the line
under the grid -- so a picture always says how much "level" it meant.
The height is compared with this avatar's in both views, so a picture of
the whole region still says who is up in a skybox.

A mark is drawn in colour when it is somebody on the friend list, and
the line under the grid says so when there is any colour in the picture
to say it about.  See "a friend is picked out in colour" below.

Two or more people in one cell are drawn as the count -- 2 to 9 -- and
ten or more as a "+", because there is no digit for ten and a 9 there
would be a lie.  Somebody standing in the same cell as this avatar is
covered by the star, which wins its own cell: a picture whose reader
cannot find themselves in it is a picture of nowhere.  The line under
the grid says how many are under the star, since somebody hidden by a
mark is exactly the person being looked for.

# A friend is picked out in colour

The question a picture is usually being read for is not "who is here"
-- who answers that -- but "where is the person I am looking for", and
most of the time that person is on the friend list.  So a friend's mark
is drawn in colour, and everybody else's is left alone.  One thing
picked out is what makes picking a thing out worth doing: the star is
never coloured, however much company it has, and neither is anybody
else.

Green unless map_friend_colour says otherwise.  The colours are named
rather than spelled as escape sequences -- black, red, green, yellow,
blue, magenta, cyan, white, and "bright" before any of them -- because
a name is a thing that can be checked and refused where it was typed,
and because "set map_friend_colour bright cyan" is a sentence.  The
line under the grid says the colour's own name, in that colour, so the
picture always names the word to type to change it.

A cell with several people in it is drawn as a count, and the count is
coloured when any one of them is a friend.  "There is a friend in that
cell" is both true and the thing being looked for; which of the two the
mark would otherwise have been is not.

Somebody outside the picture is coloured in the line that names them
underneath, for the same reason: it is the same fact about the same
person, and that line is exactly where the eye goes when the friend is
not among the marks.  Somebody standing in the same cell as this avatar
is under the star and so has no mark of their own to colour; the count
under the grid still says they are there.

The colour only ever reaches a terminal.  A picture written to a file
-- "map > listing" -- and a picture from "slsh -c" or "slsh -f" or a
piped session have no escape sequences in them at all, since a colour
in a file is not a colour, it is rubbish in the middle of the text, and
that file is usually on its way back through ". listing".  NO_COLOR is
honoured as the convention has it: set it to anything at all, "0"
included, and nothing is coloured.  When there is no colour there is no
line explaining one either, and the picture is character for character
the picture it was before there was any colour to have.

# North is up and east is right

Which is what every map does, and what the region's own coordinates
already mean: y grows northward and x eastward, so the top of the
picture is the high end of y and the right of it the high end of x.

Anybody the picture does not reach is counted and named underneath it,
with how far away they are, rather than being drawn at the edge.  An
avatar drawn at the edge is a claim about where they are standing, and
that claim would be false.  The names stop at eight and the rest are a
count, because naming forty people under a grid is "who" printed twice.

# Sixty-four metres is a judgement

Nothing chooses the region view's scale -- a region is 256 metres and
that is that -- but the close view has to cover something, and 64 metres
is a choice rather than a measurement.  It is comfortably more than the
twenty metres ordinary chat carries and rather less than the draw
distance usually is, so it holds the people somebody is actually dealing
with.  A picture that covered everything the session had been told about
would put all of them in one cell in the middle.

--span changes it for one picture, in metres across, and --rows changes
how many rows either picture is drawn in.  --region has no span to
change and says so rather than ignoring one.

# What can be set

	map_rows           how many rows a picture is drawn in
	map_span           how much ground the close picture covers, in metres
	map_ratio          the shape of a character cell, height first
	map_level          how far above or below still counts as level
	map_friend_colour  the colour a friend is drawn in

"set" lists them with the values they have now, and "set NAME VALUE"
changes one and writes it to the settings file, so that the next picture
and every picture after it is drawn that way.  --rows and --span
override the first two for one command and change nothing, which is what
a flag is for.

# It numbers nobody

Every listing that prints a number down the left -- who, friends, lookup
-- replaces what a number means for the commands that take one.  A
picture cannot be numbered usefully, since two people in one cell are
one character, so this deliberately leaves the last listing alone: after
a map, "im 2" still means the second line of whatever printed one.

# What it cannot show

The avatars are the ones the simulator has described to this session,
which is what is within the draw distance and not everyone in the
region.  So --region draws the whole region and can only mark the people
this session has been told about: an empty corner is a corner nobody has
described, not a corner with nobody in it.  Use "where" and the count
under the grid together rather than reading the blank cells as fact.

The data a mini-map in a viewer is drawn from is CoarseLocationUpdate,
which does carry every avatar in the region.  slgod reads its own entry
out of it, to keep the camera with an avatar that walked, and keeps
nothing about anybody else -- so there is nothing here to draw a whole
region from yet.  It would also be a coarser picture than this one: its
heights are one byte of four metre steps that stops at 1020 for
everything above it, which is a skybox reported as level with the roofs.

# Examples

The ground around this avatar, with somebody up a ladder, somebody in a
cellar, two people close enough together to share a cell, and one
friend among them:

	map

	around you in Pelmar Reach at 128, 128, 25
	+-------------------------------------+
	|                                     |
	|                         v           |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|             o                       |
	|                                     |
	|                    o                |
	|                  *                  |
	|                                     |
	|                ^                    |
	|                            2        |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	+-------------------------------------+
	64m x 64m; north is up, east is right
	* you   o within 3m of your height   ^ higher   v lower
	a digit is that many in one cell, and + is more than nine
	green is somebody on your friend list
	6 avatars in the picture
	1 outside it: Orla Pennywort 99m

The friend is the "o" three rows above the star and five columns to the
left of it: twelve metres north, which is three rows of four metres, and
eight west, which is a little over four and a half columns and lands in
the fifth.  On a terminal it is green and the eye finds it without being
told; this page has no colour in it, so it is said here instead.

The same moment as a picture of the region.  A cell is now a shade under
7 metres by 16 rather than about 1.7 by 4, so it reaches the
one who was too far away for the close picture and loses the difference
between this avatar and the neighbour standing a few metres from it, who
is now under the star:

	map --region

	all of Pelmar Reach at 128, 128, 25
	+-------------------------------------+
	|                                     |
	|                  o                  |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                    v                |
	|                 o*                  |
	|                 ^   2               |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	|                                     |
	+-------------------------------------+
	256m x 256m; north is up, east is right
	* you   o within 3m of your height   ^ higher   v lower
	a digit is that many in one cell, and + is more than nine
	green is somebody on your friend list
	7 avatars in the picture
	1 of them is where you are, under the *

The friend has not moved -- twelve metres north is inside one sixteen
metre row here, and eight metres west is one column -- so the same
person is the "o" immediately to the left of the star, and is the green
one again.

A bigger picture, for a terminal with the room for it, and a smaller
square of ground in more detail:

	map --rows 24
	map --span 24

See also: set for the settings above and everything else slsh keeps,
who for the same people as a listing with their distances and the
numbers other commands take, where for this avatar's own position, look
for what the region says about itself, and tp for going to a position
rather than reading one.
