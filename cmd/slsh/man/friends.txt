friends lists the people this avatar is friends with who are logged in.
It is the narrow list on purpose: a friend list of four hundred people,
of whom two are online, answers a question nobody asked.  The whole of
it is one flag away.

It takes nothing else.  Each line is a number, a name and a key, and
the listing is what the next command means by a number -- "friends"
then "im 2" is the ordinary way round, and the same number works for
profile, offer or give.

# Where the list comes from, and what it is not

Both halves of this arrive once and never again.  The list itself is
part of the login response and is on no message the simulator ever
sends afterwards; who is online arrives seconds after the handshake, as
one burst naming everybody already logged in, and after that only as
people come and go.  So it is held for the whole session rather than
asked for, and a shell that started an hour later is reading what was
caught at the time rather than asking now.

A friendship formed while the session is up is added as it happens,
since the grid tells the side that offered and tells the side that
accepted nothing whatsoever, on the grounds that it was the one that
did it.

Online is what the grid volunteered, and no more than that.  Each
friendship carries rights on both sides -- to see the other online, to
see them on the map, to edit their objects -- so this list can only be
as complete as what those rights allow, and it is not a promise that
somebody is awake or willing to be written to.

# Options

-a lists everybody rather than the ones online.  It is the list to use
when the question is who somebody has ever befriended, or when a name
is wanted for a key.

# Examples

	friends
	friends -a

Write to the second person listed:

	friends
	im 2 are you still at the build

See also: offer for making a friendship, offers for one waiting to be
answered, who for the people in the region who are not friends, and
lookup for everybody else.
