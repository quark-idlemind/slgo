parcel says what land the avatar is standing on: its name, who owns
it, how much of its prim allowance is used, and what it permits.  A
region is one place to the protocol and several places to everybody in
it -- "where" gives the region and this gives the other half.  It is
the command to reach for when building, flying, running a script or a
stream has stopped working for no reason the region explains.

With a point it describes any parcel in this region rather than the one
underfoot: "parcel 60,60" for the land two parcels over.  The avatar
does not move and nothing is asked of it; the point is metres from the
region's south west corner, the same numbers "where" prints.

# Asking is the ordinary way, and it is quick

The parcel is asked for, not remembered.  Measured on Agni, every kind
of parcel question was answered between 90 and 130 milliseconds, so
this is a question and not a wait -- and the answer is what is true
now, where anything remembered is only as new as the last arrival.  An
avatar that has stood in one place while the land was sold, renamed or
divided would otherwise be told what was true when it got there.

If the ask goes unanswered, the command falls back to what the session
was told when it arrived and says so in as many words, so that a stale
answer can never be mistaken for a fresh one.

# The whole region's layout cannot be asked for at all

ParcelOverlay arrives in four packets as the avatar arrives, and never
again.  It carries one byte for every 4 metre square of the region --
who owns it, and where the property lines run -- and no names at all.
Nothing brings it back: a request for it is answered with silence, and
the only way to fetch it again is to arrive again.

So --region and --map come from whatever held the session at that
moment.  A shell attached to a daemon that has been up for hours gets
them; a session that has just logged in and not moved may not have
them, and the command says so rather than drawing an empty region.

--region cuts the region along those property lines and then asks about
a point inside each piece, so the listing has names on it: the shapes
come from the overlay and the names from a question apiece, about a
tenth of a second each, sequentially -- fourteen parcels take two
seconds.  A region that stops answering is still listed by its shapes,
with the missing names said plainly.

Asking is also what makes the count right.  One parcel can be two
pieces of ground -- land bought either side of a road is still one
parcel -- and nothing in the overlay says so, where the local id the
answers come back with says it plainly.  Where that happens the listing
says how many pieces the parcels were cut into.

--map draws the same parcels in the shape "map" draws a region, one
character to a cell and a different character for each parcel, with a
key under it naming them.  A "?" is a quarter of the overlay that never
arrived.

Drawing who OWNS each square was the first attempt and it drew nothing:
every square of the mainland region this was measured in reads "owned",
Linden's own roads and rez zones included, so the picture was one
character from corner to corner.  Which parcel is which is the thing a
map of parcels is for.

Linden's own protected land is the exception and is drawn as ground: a
blank for the roads and waterways a region is laid out around, and a
"." for the rez zones inside them.  It is most of a mainland region by
area and none of it is anybody's, so a letter of its own would put the
loudest mark in the picture on the one parcel nobody is asking about,
and take the eye off the homes.

Each parcel is drawn as a letter, in a colour, and both matter.  The
colour is what tells two parcels apart at a glance; the letter is what
tells them apart at all, because a picture written to a file, down a
pipe, or on a terminal with NO_COLOR set has no colour in it and would
otherwise be a wash of one character.  The colours are the eight a
terminal has always had and the bright half of each, as foreground and
never as a block behind the letter, for the reasons "map" gives.

# A local id is not a name

Every parcel has two namings and neither is its name.  The local id is
the region's own numbering, printed here as "local": it is what a push
and an overlay use, it is what "parcel" would take if it took one, and
it belongs to one region -- the same number in the region next door is
a different piece of land.  The other naming is a uuid the properties
never carry at all, which the RemoteParcelRequest capability answers
with, and which arrives free beside the dwell.

# What it does to a viewer watching the same session

Asking about a parcel moves the simulator's idea of which parcel this
agent has SELECTED.  A viewer attached to this session through slgod
will notice: its About Land floater redraws to show the parcel that was
asked about, which is not the one under its avatar.  That is the grid
working as designed and not a fault, but somebody watching the screen
while somebody else types "parcel 200,200" will see the panel change
under them.

# Examples

	parcel
	parcel 60,60
	parcel --region
	parcel --map --rows 24

See also: where for the region and the position, look for what the
simulator says about the region itself, group for the membership that
parcel permissions usually hang on, and map for who is standing here.
