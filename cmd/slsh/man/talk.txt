talk lists the conversations, and marks the one a line typed in chat
mode would go to.  Chat mode shows only the current one, in its prompt;
this is the whole set tab moves between.

It takes nothing.  Open chat is always the first entry, called Local,
and each person spoken to or heard from is another, in the order they
appeared.  A star marks where typing would land.

# The numbers are positions and nothing takes them

The number in each row is where that conversation sits in the cycle, so
that a listing of six can be counted rather than squinted at.  Nothing
accepts it as an argument: the way to another conversation is tab in
chat mode, or naming the person to "im" or "chat".  A conversation that
opened while this listing was on the screen has moved the numbers under
it, which is the other reason not to type one.

The danger is that typing one is not refused.  A number given to "im",
"offer", "give" or "profile" means the last listing of people -- what
"who", "friends" or "lookup" printed, or the several people a "profile"
listed rather than guess between -- and this is not one of those: it
leaves that listing exactly as it was.  So "talk" and then "im 2"
writes to the second person of whatever was listed before, who has
every chance of being somebody else entirely, and the shell reports it
as a success because nothing about it went wrong.  With no such listing
at all it says there is no 2 in the last listing, which is the harmless
half of the same thing.

# A conversation opens by itself and does not close

The list grows when somebody sends an instant message, and a notice
says so at the moment it happens -- that notice is worth reading,
because it means tab now has one more stop on it.  It grows quietly
when this side starts one: "im WHO TEXT" that opens a conversation says
so, but "chat WHO" and "im WHO" with nothing to say simply switch to a
row that was not there before.  Nothing removes an entry; the list is
the session's memory of who has been talked to, and it ends when the
shell does.  Local cannot be closed either, which is what makes tab a
cycle: pressing it enough times always comes back to speaking out
loud.

The names can improve, but not by themselves.  An instant message
carries a key and sometimes no name at all, so a conversation can
appear as the first eight characters of a key in brackets --
(d8467e57).  Nothing here then asks the grid who that is, and a name
the grid volunteers afterwards does not reach the row: with the name
learned between one message and the next, the listing was measured
still saying (d8467e57).  What relabels it is a later message from that
person that does carry a name, or a command naming them by key -- "chat
d8467e57-..." asks the grid and puts the answer in the row.

# Examples

	talk

See also: chat for entering the mode this describes, im for opening a
conversation with somebody, and who for everybody in the region,
whether or not there is a conversation with them.
