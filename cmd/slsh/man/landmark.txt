landmark is the command for a place said by name, where tp is the
command for a place said in numbers.  A landmark is an ordinary
inventory item -- the grid's own way of writing a place down -- and
this lists the ones this avatar holds, says where one of them goes,
makes one of wherever the avatar is standing, and goes to one.

With no arguments it lists them, as paths, so that two landmarks of one
name can be told apart.  With a name it reads that one and prints where
it goes, without moving.  Going there is asked for separately, and so
is going home:

	landmark
	landmark Thrushmoor
	landmark --make Thrushmoor
	landmark --go Thrushmoor
	landmark --home

The avatar moves only in the last two.  That is deliberate.  If going
somewhere were the bare form, then reading a landmark and being
somewhere else afterwards would be one typo apart, so it is not the
bare form; and for the same reason none of the three verbs has a short
letter, because -g beside -m is exactly the typo a whole word prevents.

# A landmark is a point and not a place

What the asset holds is a region and three numbers, and that is the
whole of it -- 96 bytes of text.  It carries no region name, no parcel,
no owner and no description of anything.  So a landmark remembers where
you stood and nothing about what was there, and everything a person
would recognise about the place has to be asked for again on arrival.
"parcel" is the command that asks.

Nothing keeps the far end still.  The parcel may have been sold, the
platform stood on taken away, the region emptied or removed -- and none
of that is in the asset and none of it is said on arrival.  A landmark
into what is now empty air is an arrival in empty air followed by a
fall: one pointed at 1000 metres was measured putting the avatar on the
ground a minute later, with nothing reported by anybody.

A parcel that refuses this avatar refuses the arrival, and a teleport
refused inside a region produces no reply of any kind.  See tp, which
has the same trap for the same reason.

# Where it goes is an id and stays one

The line the reading form prints as "region" is the region's grid-wide
uuid, because that is what the asset says.  It is not a region name and
it cannot be turned into one here: the only way this program has of
finding a region is the world map's search by NAME, and a landmark
carries no name to search for.  So the honest answer is the id and the
position, and a line that printed something place-shaped beside it
would be an invention.

Going there needs no lookup at all.  The simulator turns the region id
into a handle itself, which is the whole reason this is not tp with a
map lookup in front of it.

# An item has two ids and the grid takes only one

Every inventory item has an ITEM id and an ASSET id.  "ls -l" prints
the first, and the second is the one every landmark message takes.
They are both uuids and nothing about either says which it is.

Sent the wrong one, the grid says nothing.  Measured on Agni: the item
id, and a uuid that is nothing at all, were each answered with perfect
silence -- no start, no progress, no refusal -- waited out to twelve
seconds apiece.  There is no error to report and nothing to catch, so a
person who typed a landmark and watched nothing happen has no way of
telling a wrong id from a grid that is thinking about it.

That is why a name here means something in inventory and never a bare
uuid handed on to the grid: the asset id comes out of the listing,
which is the one place the two are told apart correctly.  A uuid may be
typed, and it is looked up in that same listing as either of an item's
two ids; a uuid this avatar does not hold is refused rather than sent.

The one wrong id the grid is not silent about is the null one, which it
reads as HOME and obeys in about a second.  So a landmark with no asset
id in it is refused here rather than being allowed to become a quiet
trip home.

A link to a landmark is not a landmark, and is left out of the listing
for the same reason: a link's asset id is the ITEM id of the thing it
points at, so following one would produce exactly that silence.  Name
the landmark itself.

# What is in the trash is not reached from here

rm in this shell moves things to the trash rather than destroying them,
so any inventory that has been used has deleted landmarks in it -- two
of the seven in the avatar this was written against.  A deleted
landmark still has a name, still has an asset and would still teleport
perfectly well, which is the problem: without a rule it would be listed
beside the kept ones, and one of them made a live landmark ambiguous
with its own corpse.

So the trash is left out.  Nothing in it is listed, and nothing in it
can be named -- not by its name, not by its whole path, and not by
either of its ids.  A form that reached in would mean going somewhere
could still take the avatar to a landmark somebody deleted, which is
the whole of what leaving them out is for, and a rule that read
"deleted, unless you type more of it" is not one anybody can hold in
their head.  "mv" moves a landmark back out of the trash, and then it
is a landmark again.

Left out is not the same as unmentioned.  A name whose only answer is
in the trash is told so, and told where it is, because a person who
deleted it an hour ago and has forgotten is owed the sentence rather
than "no landmark called that" -- and a listing with nothing in it says
how many of them are deleted.

The trash is found by its folder type and not by its name.  It can be
renamed, and an account made through a viewer in another language never
called it "Trash" in the first place.

# Making one is the simulator's answer and not this shell's

Nothing says where the landmark is of, and nothing can.  The position
is written by the simulator out of where it believes this avatar is at
the instant the message lands, so for a walking avatar it is not quite
the place it was asked from, and what is printed afterwards is read
back out of the asset rather than assumed.  Measured, it agrees with
where the avatar stood to a couple of centimetres, which is a standing
avatar's drift rather than a rounding.

No folder is named on the way out.  The simulator files a new landmark
under Landmarks without being asked, which is where a viewer puts them
too, and naming a folder would only be a way of getting it wrong.  The
item arrives in about 300 milliseconds and its asset is readable about
200 after that, so the reading back costs one short pause at worst; if
it will not read, the item is made anyway and the command says so.

A name is not made unique by anything.  Two landmarks called the same
thing are two landmarks called the same thing, and naming one of them
afterwards is refused with both listed -- say which by its whole path,
or by the id printed beside it.

# What a teleport to one costs

About a second.  Three measured trips -- within one region, across two,
and to a landmark made a moment earlier -- each took 1.0 seconds, and
nothing had to be looked up in front of any of them.

The wait is thirty seconds, which is tp's, and it can be set with
--wait, in seconds.  The name is printed before the waiting starts,
because a shell that has silently stopped answering is
indistinguishable from one that has hung, and what is printed after the
arrival is where the avatar actually ended up, read back.

Everything the shell knew about the region it left is dropped on the
way, exactly as it is for tp: local ids are the region's own numbering
and the next region hands the same numbers out again.

# Home is the one landmark nobody has to own

Going home is the same message with the null id in it, which the grid
reads as home rather than as an error.  It costs no inventory lookup
and needs no item, so it works for an avatar that has never made a
landmark in its life.  Home is an account setting; nothing here can
read it or change it, and going there is the only way to find out where
it is.

# Examples

	landmark
	landmark Thrushmoor
	landmark /Landmarks/Thrushmoor
	landmark --make Pelmar Reach Workshop
	landmark --go Thrushmoor
	landmark --wait 60 --go Thrushmoor
	landmark --home

A name with spaces in it needs no quoting: the words after the flags
are joined back together, as tp joins a region name.

See also: tp for a place said in numbers and for what a teleport costs
and how it is refused, where for the position a landmark is made from,
parcel for what the land at the far end turns out to be, ls for the
items themselves and the two ids each of them has, mv for taking one
back out of the trash, and regions for finding a place by name when
there is no landmark to it.
